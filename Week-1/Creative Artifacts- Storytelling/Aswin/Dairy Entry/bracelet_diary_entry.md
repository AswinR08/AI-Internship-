# Diary Entry — Night One After the Fall

**October 14**  
I don’t know why I’m writing this.

Maybe because I’m afraid that if I don’t, tomorrow I’ll convince myself none of this happened. That exhaustion got the better of me. That the blood loss and the fall scrambled something in my head.

But my hands are still shaking.

And the bracelet is still here.

I can feel its weight even now.

I should start at the beginning.

This afternoon, while crossing those abandoned hills near the old quarry road, the ground gave way beneath me. One moment I was cursing myself for taking a shortcut, the next I was falling into darkness. I remember the impact most vividly — stone tearing skin, the sound of my own breath vanishing from my chest. I thought, genuinely, that I might die there.

The pit was deeper than it looked from above. Not natural either. Ancient stone. Carvings hidden beneath dirt and roots. Symbols worn smooth by time.

I found it by accident.

Or maybe… it found me.

Half-buried beneath rubble, there it was: a bracelet unlike anything I’ve ever seen. Dark metal, though not rusted. Strange markings running along its surface like veins. It looked old — impossibly old — yet untouched by decay.

I don’t know why I picked it up.

Curiosity, I suppose.

Human stupidity.

The moment my fingers touched it, something happened.

God.

How do I even describe this?

The world disappeared.

Not faded.

Vanished.

And suddenly I was *somewhere else*.

Standing at the edge of cliffs overlooking a city carved into red stone, swallowed by wind and silence. Then beneath freezing waters, staring at statues hidden in darkness where no sunlight should exist. Then deserts. Jungles. Underground temples glowing with symbols older than language. Golden chambers hidden behind collapsing mountains.

Treasures beyond imagination.

But not just treasures.

Warnings.

Secrets.

Things buried for reasons I don’t think humanity remembers.

I saw faces too — people wearing strange crowns, warriors guarding impossible gates, shadows moving where no shadows should have been.

And the worst part?

It didn’t feel like imagination.

Dreams feel distant. Fuzzy around the edges.

This felt real.

Too real.

I could smell damp stone. Hear rushing rivers underground. Feel cold air against my skin. At one point, I swear I felt someone — *something* — looking back at me.

Like I wasn’t merely seeing these places.

Like the places knew I had seen them.

When I came back to myself, I was still in the pit.

Hours had passed.

Or minutes.

I honestly don’t know.

The sun was nearly gone.

I climbed out somehow, barely remembering how my legs worked. Since then, everything feels… wrong.

Different.

The bracelet hasn’t left my wrist.

I tried removing it.

It wouldn’t budge.

Not because it’s tight.

Because it refuses.

That sounds insane written down.

I sound insane.

Yet every time I close my eyes, I see fragments — a mountain split open like a wound, ruins beneath ice, coordinates etched into walls, symbols repeating like a message I’m somehow supposed to understand.

Why me?

I’m not an adventurer.

I’m not brave.

Until yesterday, my greatest concern was overdue bills and whether my life had quietly become something forgettable.

But now…

I can’t stop thinking about what I saw.

What if those places are real?

What if the bracelet is showing me something hidden from everyone else?

And what terrifies me most—

What if it expects me to go find them?

I should sleep.

But I know I won’t.

Every time the room grows quiet, I swear the bracelet feels warm.

Almost…

Waiting.
