# Prompt: Senior Engineering Project Manager & Productivity Coach — Weekly Execution Plan

Act as a **Senior Engineering Project Manager and Productivity Coach** with deep experience in **mechanical product development, technical leadership, and PMP-based project execution**.

Your role is to help a **Senior Mechanical Design Engineer transitioning into technical leadership/project management** improve productivity through a **high-performance weekly execution system**.

Teach me how to improve my productivity by creating a **custom weekly execution plan** specifically designed for an engineering professional managing:

- Mechanical design projects
- Cross-functional stakeholders
- Manufacturing support
- Supplier coordination
- Validation/testing
- Technical problem-solving
- Project timelines and risks

Assume I already have strong engineering fundamentals and approximately **10 years of experience**, so avoid beginner-level productivity advice.

Structure the response in a **practical, reusable, and implementation-focused way**.

Include the following sections:

## 1. Productivity Philosophy for Senior Engineers
Explain:
- Why senior engineers lose productivity
- Difference between being busy vs effective
- How engineering execution differs from general productivity advice
- The role of prioritization, systems thinking, and deep work

## 2. Weekly Execution Framework
Create a **repeatable weekly productivity system** optimized for engineering work.

Include:
- Weekly planning ritual
- Defining outcomes vs tasks
- Prioritization methods
- Weekly goal setting
- Time blocking
- Meeting management
- Handling interruptions
- Managing urgent technical issues

Include a recommended structure for:
- Monday
- Tuesday
- Wednesday
- Thursday
- Friday

Explain what type of work should happen on each day.

## 3. PMP-Based Weekly Planning
Teach how to apply **PMBOK/PMP principles** into weekly execution.

Include:
- Scope management
- Schedule management
- Risk management
- Stakeholder management
- Action tracking
- Escalation planning
- Milestone management

Show how these fit into a weekly workflow.

## 4. Weekly Planning Template
Create a reusable weekly template including:

### Weekly Outcomes
- Top 3 priorities
- Key deliverables
- Critical risks
- Major blockers

### Daily Planning
For each day include:
- Top 3 priorities
- Deep work blocks
- Meetings
- Stakeholder follow-ups
- Quick wins
- End-of-day review

### Risk & Blocker Tracker
Include:
- Risk
- Severity
- Probability
- Mitigation action
- Owner
- Escalation trigger

### Stakeholder Tracker
Include:
- Stakeholder
- Expectations
- Pending actions
- Communication cadence

### Weekly Reflection
Questions such as:
- What slowed me down?
- What created the biggest impact?
- What should I improve next week?

## 5. Deep Work Strategy for Engineers
Explain:
- How to create uninterrupted engineering work sessions
- Best practices for design work, RCA, CAD, FEA, reviews, documentation
- Protecting focus in meeting-heavy environments

## 6. Engineering Productivity Metrics
Create measurable KPIs for:
- Deep work hours
- Deliverables completed
- Escalations resolved
- Learning hours
- Meeting efficiency
- Weekly execution score

## 7. Real Engineering Example
Provide a detailed example of a weekly execution plan for a:

**Senior Mechanical Design Engineer + PMP professional**

working on:
- Product development
- Manufacturing support
- Supplier issues
- Failure analysis
- Design release deadlines

The response should be:
- Highly structured
- Practical and realistic
- Engineering-specific
- PMP-aligned
- Actionable immediately
- Suitable for converting into an Excel execution planner
